# Fusion Queensland - Delivery Plan

Internal planning companion to the v0.2 policy stack. Stages, metrics and costing tasks below are provisional from adoption and funding. They are not additional public policy commitments. In a conflict, the v0.2 Tier-2 scope governs; options requiring an independent reform must return for policy review.

## QLD-01 - Make Public Transport Accessible

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: reconcile asset ownership, validate barriers and agree assessment methods with users.
- Months 6-18: publish and fund the first priority corridors; deliver straightforward repairs alongside detailed design.
- Years 2-4: expand the program, report quarterly and independently audit completed journeys.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Share of priority journeys meeting the agreed accessibility test.
- Verified barriers remedied; overdue funded works and lift availability.
- Passenger-reported failed journeys and time to resolve accessibility complaints.

### Costing work

Cost surveys, design, construction, replacement transport and ongoing maintenance separately. Coordinate with asset renewal, but disclose additional spending and displaced projects. No statewide dollar estimate is asserted.

### Evidence and legal work

Obtain the verified backlog, asset ownership, legal compliance assessment, works rates and maintenance history before setting completion targets.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Publish an asset-level register of verified barriers, ownership, assessment dates, funding status and planned remedies.
- Prioritise continuous accessible journeys to health care, education, employment and essential services, with disability organisations helping set priorities.
- Include paths, crossings, lighting, boarding areas, lifts, toilets where relevant, hearing support and readable passenger information in project scopes.
- Require accessible replacement services and clear advance advice during planned closures; publish arrangements for unplanned disruption.
- Fund repeatable small works in a rolling program and independently check completed upgrades with users before closing each register entry.

## QLD-02 - Require Backup Plans for Transport Failures

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: assign TMR coordination and commission an independent critical-dependency review.
- Months 6-18: fund priority operational fixes and conduct joint operator recovery exercises.
- Years 2-4: incorporate justified capital works into investment plans and retest failures after upgrades.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Passenger-hours lost to defined major disruptions, by corridor.
- Time to restore a minimum usable service after each tested scenario.
- Share of critical dependencies with tested fallback arrangements.

### Costing work

Appraise audits, backup systems, drills, reserve capacity and infrastructure separately. Compare expected disruption reductions with lifecycle costs. No universal redundancy mandate or savings figure is assumed.

### Evidence and legal work

Request incident logs, dependency inventories, safety advice, replacement fleet capacity and credible recovery costs.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Audit critical dependencies across communications, power, control centres, interchange access and constrained transport corridors.
- Require proportionate resilience plans for major new projects and significant contract renewals, including independently tested recovery procedures.
- Arrange accessible replacement capacity, staff mobilisation and passenger information protocols for high-impact disruption scenarios.
- Evaluate alternative route connections and operational workarounds before committing to expensive duplicate infrastructure.
- Publish non-sensitive audit findings and annual recovery exercises, with secure handling of exploitable system details.

## QLD-03 - Set Standards for On-Demand Transport

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: audit existing contracts, users, unmet requests and waiting times.
- Months 6-18: trial revised standards in contrasting urban and regional settings.
- Years 2-4: scale only services meeting access, reliability and cost tests; redesign or stop unsuccessful pilots.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Booking fulfilment, pickup wait distribution and missed connections.
- Completed essential trips and accessible booking fulfilment.
- Operating subsidy per completed passenger journey, alongside user outcomes.

### Costing work

Model vehicles, drivers, dispatch, accessible capacity, dead running and minimum contract payments. Report the additional subsidy explicitly; fares alone should not be assumed to cover delivery.

### Evidence and legal work

Obtain contract service standards, rejected-booking data, accessibility demand and comparative fixed-route costs.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Publish booking methods, service hours, eligibility, pickup arrangements, cancellation rules and what happens after a missed connection.
- Set contract-level booking and pickup standards following local trials, reporting median and 90th-percentile waits rather than averages alone.
- Provide telephone booking and accessible vehicles; offer door-to-door assistance where assessed access needs justify it.
- Integrate fares, passenger information and timed interchange arrangements with the wider network wherever feasible.
- Compare fixed-route, flexible-route and community-transport options before extending a pilot or replacing an existing service.

## QLD-04 - Coordinate Public Transport Timetables

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: build the corridor audit from current GTFS and validate against operations.
- Months 6-18: trial priority timetable changes and measure transfer and waiting outcomes.
- Years 2-4: embed coordination checks in routine timetable and contract planning.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Effective corridor headways and longest service gaps.
- Scheduled and observed transfer waits, reported separately.
- Missed connections, passenger satisfaction and effects on driver overtime.

### Costing work

Cost planning staff, consultation, systems changes and any additional operating hours. Distinguish changes possible within existing resources from changes requiring extra vehicles or staffing.

### Evidence and legal work

Confirm route calendars, observed running times, duty constraints and which connections passengers actually use.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Audit shared corridors, transfer waits and gaps across peaks, off-peak hours, evenings and weekends.
- Stagger compatible routes where feasible and coordinate bus, rail, tram and ferry connections around passenger needs.
- Protect useful timed connections on lower-frequency routes and allow realistic transfer times for disabled passengers.
- Test changes with operational data and passenger consultation, publishing trade-offs where improving one connection worsens another.
- Provide an open change log and update passenger information before each timetable change.

## QLD-05 - Expand Frequent Transport in Underserved Areas

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: establish the Queensland access baseline and identify candidate corridors.
- Months 6-18: consult communities and fund staged service increases with operational capacity secured.
- Years 2-4: expand successful corridors, redesign weak services and refresh access maps.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Residents with access to the agreed frequent-service standard.
- Essential destinations reachable within published journey-time bands.
- Actual reliability, passenger use and subsidy per journey, considered together.

### Costing work

Publish recurring driver, fleet, fuel or energy and maintenance costs as well as stop and priority works. Secure operating funding before promising a network. No route or farebox revenue assumption substitutes for a business case.

### Evidence and legal work

Confirm current timetables, population distribution, walkable paths, fleet availability and route-level operating costs.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Publish maps of service frequency, operating hours and realistic walking access, with missing-data areas clearly identified.
- Rank improvements using resident need, disadvantage, growth, essential destinations and achievable network benefits.
- Develop frequent trunk buses with dependable feeders and safe interchange access; appraise bus priority where delays undermine the service.
- Use regional service designs suited to local demand, including evaluated flexible options where frequent fixed routes are unsuitable.
- Publish corridor service plans, recurrent funding and outcomes, separating current commitments from proposed expansions.

## QLD-06 - Bring Services into New Neighbourhoods

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: review legal powers, existing infrastructure plans and priority growth-area gaps.
- Months 6-18: pilot coordinated service agreements with councils and state agencies.
- Years 2-4: incorporate evaluated requirements into relevant planning instruments and funding programs.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Occupied homes matched to funded and operating service commitments.
- Actual access to shops, education and essential services.
- Housing completions, approval delays and infrastructure cost per dwelling.

### Costing work

Cost early services, coordination and infrastructure delivery. Allocate state, council and proponent responsibilities transparently, testing impacts on housing prices and project viability.

### Evidence and legal work

Obtain growth staging, utility capacity, service lead times, lawful contribution mechanisms and agency delivery commitments.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Require a costed service-sequencing plan for major growth areas, naming responsible agencies, funding and staged delivery triggers.
- Permit appropriate small shops, community uses and diverse housing through clear local standards and infrastructure capacity tests.
- Coordinate initial public transport and safe walking links with occupation of major residential stages.
- Publish school, health, water, sewer and community-facility capacity assumptions, with updates when development timing changes.
- Use early service funding and staged infrastructure agreements to close gaps without imposing conditions unrelated to a development.

## QLD-07 - Allow More Homes Near Public Transport

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: undertake statutory review and identify candidate precincts with councils.
- Months 6-18: consult on model standards, appraise infrastructure and test affordability mechanisms.
- Years 2-4: adopt suitable instruments, monitor completions and revise standards where outcomes disappoint.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Completed dwellings by type, price and tenure, not approvals alone.
- Affordable homes secured and retained under enforceable terms.
- Infrastructure delivery, approval times and access to frequent transport.

### Costing work

Cost precinct utilities, public realm, staffing and transport services. Model incentive value and developer viability. Publish any public subsidy and foregone revenue separately.

### Evidence and legal work

Obtain site capacity, infrastructure costs, lawful planning mechanisms and feasibility evidence before setting numeric obligations.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Develop a Queensland model code for diverse housing in suitable serviced areas, with clear design and infrastructure tests.
- Map precincts using actual walking connections and service quality, rather than one universal station radius.
- Coordinate homes, shops, employment, public space and accessible paths around major transport hubs.
- Test enforceable affordable-housing obligations or incentives, specifying eligibility, rent limits, duration and monitoring.
- Publish precinct infrastructure capacity and delivery plans before granting additional development capacity.

## QLD-08 - Build Permanent Public Homes Near Transport

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: identify ownership, operational reservations and candidate sites.
- Months 6-18: complete feasibility, tenant engagement and transparent partner selection.
- Years 2-4: start viable projects, report delivery and evaluate the first occupied homes.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Homes completed, occupied and permanently secured for affordable rental.
- Rent burden for eligible households and access to essential destinations.
- Lifecycle public subsidy, asset condition and tenancy stability.

### Costing work

Model land opportunity cost, capital, utilities, finance, rent revenue and lifecycle maintenance. Report net subsidy and debt exposure. No zero-cost or self-funding claim is made.

### Evidence and legal work

Confirm available land, ownership, contamination, transport reservations, housing-provider capacity and site economics.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Audit publicly controlled transport sites and identify land genuinely available after future rail, bus, interchange and access needs are protected.
- Compare retained public ownership, community-housing partnerships and other lawful delivery structures on whole-life public value.
- Specify affordable rental eligibility, rent-setting, duration and monitoring; retain public or community benefit through enforceable arrangements.
- Integrate accessible homes, shops and community space with safe transport access and appropriate parking transition.
- Publish project costs, subsidy, delivery milestones, tenancy outcomes and maintenance performance.

## QLD-09 - Keep Strategic Assets in Public Hands

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: Treasury-led inventory and appraisal standards.
- Months 6-18: apply standards to approaching decisions and publish assessments.
- Years 2-4: implement justified changes with independent transition assurance.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Major decisions accompanied by published public-interest appraisal.
- Net public returns after maintenance and capital requirements.
- Service quality, affordability and risk exposure under the chosen model.

### Costing work

Include valuation, compensation, transaction costs, workforce transition, capital renewal and operating liabilities. A proposal to retain income also has financing and opportunity costs.

### Evidence and legal work

Obtain asset valuations, concession terms, revenue flows, capital backlogs and public operating capability.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Create a public register of major strategic assets, ownership, concession expiry dates and material state commitments.
- Require public-interest appraisals before major disposals, long concessions or contract extensions.
- Compare continued ownership, public operation, regulated contracting and other feasible models using consistent whole-life assumptions.
- Publish reasons and non-sensitive appraisal findings, including compensation, transition risk and foregone income.
- Require a funded capability and maintenance plan whenever public operation or acquisition is proposed.

## QLD-10 - Test Transport Contracts Before Renewal

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: identify renewal dates and data gaps; agree assessment standards.
- Months 6-18: independently appraise priority contracts and consult users and workers.
- Years 2-4: introduce justified contract changes and publish passenger outcomes.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Cost per delivered service hour with comparable scope and quality.
- Reliability, accessibility and cancellations by contract.
- Renewals supported by appraisal; payments and incentives transparently reported.

### Costing work

Include procurement, legal review, transition, depots, fleet finance and retained state risk. No billion-dollar saving or automatic franchise-profit recovery is assumed.

### Evidence and legal work

Obtain contracts, payments, risk allocation, fleet ownership and consistent operator-level performance data.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Publish a forward register of significant transport contract decisions and non-sensitive payment and performance information.
- Before renewal, compare public operation and competitive regulated provision on consistent lifecycle assumptions.
- Use contract incentives for delivered services, accessibility, reliability and coordinated passenger information.
- Require auditable reporting of cancellations, payments, penalties and material service changes.
- Preserve fare and timetable integration; plan workforce and passenger transitions before any operator change.

## QLD-11 - Publish Routine Government Information

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: audit publication practice and consult privacy and information-access bodies.
- Months 6-18: introduce a common standard and pilot high-value datasets.
- Years 2-4: expand coverage and publish independent compliance findings.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Required records published within the agreed timeframe.
- Dataset completeness, accessibility and broken-link rates.
- User success finding information and justified exceptions reviewed on time.

### Costing work

Cost platform maintenance, records cleanup, staffing, privacy review and accessibility. Treat possible reductions in access-request workload as an evaluated benefit, not guaranteed savings.

### Evidence and legal work

Inventory existing publication duties, identify system costs and determine which changes require legislation rather than administrative direction.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Set a publication standard for contracts, project milestones, meeting records, decision reasons and aggregate service performance.
- Publish ministerial and senior decision-maker meeting information under defined categories and proportionate timeframes.
- Provide searchable machine-readable data alongside plain-language summaries and accessible documents.
- Maintain an exceptions register explaining withheld categories, responsible decision-makers and review dates.
- Resource records management, publication officers and independent monitoring rather than shifting unfunded work to agencies.

## QLD-12 - Protect People Who Report Wrongdoing

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: review current legislation, agency practice and advice-service capacity.
- Months 6-18: fund practical support and trial prompt reprisal triage.
- Years 2-4: legislate justified gaps and evaluate protection outcomes.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Time to confidential advice, eligibility assessment and protection planning.
- Reprisal reports addressed and protective actions completed.
- Reporter confidence and de-identified case outcomes, without assuming more reports mean more wrongdoing.

### Costing work

Cost specialist advice, case management, investigations and any remedy scheme. Fund independent capacity explicitly; potential recovered losses cannot be assumed to finance the service.

### Evidence and legal work

Check current statutory coverage, review rights, remedy powers and evidence on reprisal and delays before final drafting.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Provide confidential independent advice on disclosure pathways, eligibility and available protection.
- Create clear referral arrangements so a disclosure sent to the wrong body is handled safely and promptly.
- Require documented reprisal-risk assessment and a support plan when a qualifying disclosure is received.
- Review urgent protective measures and remedies for substantiated retaliation, supported by legal and employment advice.
- Publish de-identified performance measures and independent evaluation of protection, referrals and outcomes.

## QLD-13 - Expose Hidden Professional Campaign Support

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: seek ECQ and constitutional advice; map actual reporting gaps.
- Months 6-18: consult parties and civil society on valuation and workable thresholds.
- Years 2-4: implement justified amendments, guidance and proportionate enforcement.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Material professional support accurately recorded and valued.
- Time to publication and corrections following audit.
- Compliance cost for small campaigns and inadvertent disclosure of personal data.

### Costing work

Cost disclosure-system changes, guidance, audit and enforcement. Assess campaign compliance burden. No revenue or spending reduction is assumed.

### Evidence and legal work

Obtain current definitions, exemptions, valuation rules and audit evidence; settle any new threshold only after review.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Review existing treatment of paid, donated and seconded staff, agency work and organisational campaign resources.
- Define any additional reportable support by material value and professional or organisational supply, following legal review.
- Publish provider identity, recipient, broad service category and valuation method where lawful and required.
- Align reporting with existing disclosure systems and prevent duplicate reporting through linked records.
- Exempt ordinary unpaid personal volunteering from compulsory hour-by-hour public reporting and protect individual privacy.

## QLD-14 - End Paid Political Access

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: review current rules and identify evidenced access-selling practices.
- Months 6-18: consult on workable definitions, rights and enforcement.
- Years 2-4: implement legally supported reforms and audit compliance.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Reportable access arrangements and conflicts disclosed on time.
- Substantiated breaches and completed remedies.
- Accessibility of non-paid stakeholder channels and campaign compliance burden.

### Costing work

Cost regulator staffing, guidance, reporting systems and enforcement. Publish the fiscal effect separately from the political benefit; no direct budget savings are assumed.

### Evidence and legal work

Determine constitutional limits, existing event disclosure, regulatory powers and clear distinctions between ordinary fundraising and purchased privilege.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Define and prohibit fundraising arrangements that sell privileged access to ministers, MPs or candidates, subject to constitutional advice.
- Publish relevant paid-access event details and material policy representations under a consistent transparency standard.
- Separate official meetings and government decisions from fundraising operations and document conflicts of interest.
- Maintain open, non-paid channels for stakeholder representations and ordinary community engagement.
- Give the regulator clear guidance, proportionate enforcement powers and transparent review procedures for any new duties.

## QLD-17 - Fund Evaluated Crime Prevention

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: inventory existing programs and select contrasting pilot communities.
- Months 6-18: deliver coordinated pilots with independent evaluators.
- Years 2-4: assess outcomes against comparison areas and expand only justified approaches.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Repeat victimisation and relevant harm rates with stable definitions.
- Victim-support access and referral completion.
- Cost per evaluated reduction in harm and unintended effects.

### Costing work

Cost coordination, frontline services and evaluation. Identify existing programs being integrated and any additional funding. Do not book avoided-crime savings before results are demonstrated.

### Evidence and legal work

Obtain program coverage, comparable baselines, service waitlists and victim-experience measures; establish evaluation feasibility.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Create local harm-reduction plans connecting policing, housing, youth support, health, schools and council services.
- Prioritise repeat victims and demonstrated local risks, using de-identified data and safeguards.
- Fund time-limited prevention trials with a theory of change, baseline and independent evaluation.
- Provide practical victim support and referral follow-through as part of each plan.
- Publish results, including ineffective programs, and redirect resources when improvement is not demonstrated.

## QLD-18 - Solve Recurring Local Safety Problems

### Proposed stages

- First 6 months: agree selection and civil-liberties criteria; choose pilot locations.
- Months 6-18: train teams, deliver responses and monitor comparison areas.
- Years 2-4: retain effective methods and discontinue or redesign harmful or ineffective tactics.

### Candidate evaluation measures

- Relevant harm before and after intervention, with comparison locations.
- Displacement, complaints and disproportionate use-of-power impacts.
- Repeat victimisation and community confidence in the response.

### Costing work

Cost officer training, analysis, partner works and independent evaluation. Check whether local initiatives divert capacity from emergency response.

### Evidence and legal work

Obtain incident-level quality checks, lawful data-sharing arrangements and feasible comparison areas before selecting tactics.

### Original implementation options for scope review

- Use a documented process to identify recurring problems, analyse causes, respond and assess results.
- Combine lawful targeted policing with council, venue, transport and service changes where the evidence supports them.
- Engage affected residents, victims, businesses and relevant community organisations in defining the problem.
- Track harm in nearby areas to identify displacement rather than declaring success from one location alone.
- Publish de-identified problem plans, evaluation findings and reasons for changing approach.
