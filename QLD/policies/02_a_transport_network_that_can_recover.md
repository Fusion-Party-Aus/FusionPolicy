# Require Backup Plans for Transport Failures

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-02  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC SERVICES

Critical transport services would be subject to independently tested resilience requirements covering essential dependencies, recovery arrangements and accessible replacement services, with non-sensitive findings published.

### The Problem

Network resilience requires more than an extra route on a map. Communications, power, control systems, staffing and accessible replacement capacity all matter. Queensland should test vulnerabilities before deciding which investments produce the greatest reduction in disruption.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Appraise audits, backup systems, drills, reserve capacity and infrastructure separately. Compare expected disruption reductions with lifecycle costs. No universal redundancy mandate or savings figure is assumed.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Transport operations and capital programs, subject to a resilience investment appraisal. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Critical dependencies** — Audit critical dependencies across communications, power, control centres, interchange access and constrained transport corridors.
- **Resilience requirements** — Require proportionate resilience plans for major new projects and significant contract renewals, including independently tested recovery procedures.
- **Replacement capacity** — Arrange accessible replacement capacity, staff mobilisation and passenger information protocols for high-impact disruption scenarios.
- **Proportionate investment** — Evaluate alternative route connections and operational workarounds before committing to expensive duplicate infrastructure.
- **Public findings** — Publish non-sensitive audit findings and annual recovery exercises, with secure handling of exploitable system details.

**Design Rationale**

Communications, power and operational dependencies can transmit a local failure across a wider service. Resilience requirements need to distinguish safe recovery from unrestricted continued operation. Duplicate infrastructure is not the only response: assessed fallback arrangements may address a vulnerability without replicating an entire corridor.

**System Interaction**

The requirements apply to dependencies capable of materially interrupting essential services. Rail safety remains a binding constraint on recovery options. Public accountability can disclose assessment coverage and recovery performance while withholding exploitable security details. A tested fallback can still offer lower capacity than normal operation; that capacity difference is a residual service trade-off even where the arrangements operate as designed.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Backup arrangements have an ongoing carrying cost even when rarely used. Investment appraisal must compare recovery capacity with the service disruption it addresses.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Rail safety remains paramount: a fallback is not permission to operate unsafely. Publish accountability without exposing cybersecurity vulnerabilities. Include regional and disabled passengers in exercises.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Translink publishes schedule and real-time data that can support service monitoring. These feeds do not themselves establish the resilience of Queensland infrastructure. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Request incident logs, dependency inventories, safety advice, replacement fleet capacity and credible recovery costs.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: assign TMR coordination and commission an independent critical-dependency review. Months 6-18: fund priority operational fixes and conduct joint operator recovery exercises. Years 2-4: incorporate justified capital works into investment plans and retest failures after upgrades. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
