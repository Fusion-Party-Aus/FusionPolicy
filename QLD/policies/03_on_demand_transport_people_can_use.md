# Set Standards for On-Demand Transport

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-03  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC SERVICES

State-supported on-demand transport contracts would specify booking access, pickup performance, service hours, accessible travel and interchange obligations, with published performance reporting and evaluation before renewal or expansion.

### The Problem

Flexible transport should provide a usable connection rather than an uncertain booking. Standards must reflect local geography, disability access and operating costs. Fixed routes should remain the preferred option where demand supports reliable frequent service.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Model vehicles, drivers, dispatch, accessible capacity, dead running and minimum contract payments. Report the additional subsidy explicitly; fares alone should not be assumed to cover delivery.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Public transport operating contracts and any approved local trial allocation. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Clear service information** — Publish booking methods, service hours, eligibility, pickup arrangements, cancellation rules and what happens after a missed connection.
- **Booking and pickup standards** — Set contract-level booking and pickup standards following local trials, reporting median and 90th-percentile waits rather than averages alone.
- **Accessible booking** — Provide telephone booking and accessible vehicles; offer door-to-door assistance where assessed access needs justify it.
- **Connected journeys** — Integrate fares, passenger information and timed interchange arrangements with the wider network wherever feasible.
- **Compare service models** — Compare fixed-route, flexible-route and community-transport options before extending a pilot or replacing an existing service.

### Research behind this policy

Translink operates a Queensland network with published schedule information. The performance of each on-demand service needs a separate local assessment; Victorian booking experiences are not Queensland evidence.

**Design Rationale**

An on-demand label does not identify the service actually available. Booking channels, geographic coverage and pickup arrangements define who can use it. Evaluation must compare equivalent travel needs rather than treating low expenditure per booking as sufficient evidence of useful access.

**System Interaction**

Contract standards distinguish ordinary zone-based travel from assistance justified by a passenger's assessed accessibility needs. Telephone access is part of booking access, not an optional separate digital-inclusion program. Fixed routes and flexible services remain alternative operating models. Variable demand and long travel distances can leave a capacity-versus-waiting-time trade-off even when an operator meets its contractual obligations.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Demand-responsive services face vehicle, driver and dispatch costs even when bookings are sparse. Cost per journey must be considered alongside whether essential travel is actually available.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Avoid app-only access. Protect booking histories. Do not remove fixed services until a replacement passes a published access test and transition support is available.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Translink operates a Queensland network with published schedule information. The performance of each on-demand service needs a separate local assessment; Victorian booking experiences are not Queensland evidence. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Obtain contract service standards, rejected-booking data, accessibility demand and comparative fixed-route costs.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: audit existing contracts, users, unmet requests and waiting times. Months 6-18: trial revised standards in contrasting urban and regional settings. Years 2-4: scale only services meeting access, reliability and cost tests; redesign or stop unsuccessful pilots. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
