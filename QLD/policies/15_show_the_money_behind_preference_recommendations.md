# Expose Money Behind Preference Recommendations

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-15  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY

Electoral disclosure rules would require public reporting of material payments and in-kind support attached to preference recommendations that are not already adequately disclosed, using linked records and election-period publication requirements.

### The Problem

Preference advice should not conceal purchased support. Queensland should make material payments and in-kind arrangements visible without implying that party recommendations control a voter's ballot or importing Victoria's upper-house electoral rules.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Cost ECQ systems, public education and enforcement. Financial arrangements should be reported consistently with existing obligations rather than creating multiple incompatible registers.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Electoral administration, public education and compliance allocations. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Review existing disclosure** — Review disclosure of payments and in-kind support linked to preference recommendations, campaign cooperation and how-to-vote materials.
- **Report material arrangements** — Require clear reporting of material arrangements not already adequately captured, using linked records to avoid duplication.
- **Publish before polling** — Publish the parties involved, nature and value of support and relevant recommendation before polling where legally feasible.
- **Protect voter choice** — Provide plain-language voter education on personal choice and distinguish recommendations from completed ballot preferences.
- **Audit and enforce** — Audit declared arrangements and provide correction, appeal and enforcement procedures.

**Design Rationale**

Financial support attached to a recommendation can be obscured if it is recorded only as unrelated campaign assistance. The design concerns transparency of material arrangements. It does not treat a recommendation as control over a voter's completed ballot.

**System Interaction**

Queensland's one-chamber Parliament differs from Victoria's electoral structure. Existing disclosure obligations define the starting point for any additional reporting. The same material arrangement should not appear as multiple independent gifts. Lawful cooperation and political expression remain within the scope boundary. Late agreements can still leave voters little time to consider a disclosure, even where a short publication requirement is met.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Linked reporting can make the financial basis of preference advice visible without creating incompatible registers. Regulator costs and campaign compliance burdens need assessment.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Protect lawful cooperation and political expression. Do not assume undisclosed wrongdoing from preference advice alone. Retain privacy and procedural fairness.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Queensland has one parliamentary chamber and existing gift disclosure obligations. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Confirm current electoral provisions, valuation rules, constitutional advice and administratively feasible election-period publication.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: map current duties and identify genuine gaps with ECQ advice. Months 6-18: consult on definitions and reporting timetables. Years 2-4: introduce justified reforms and evaluate voter understanding and compliance. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
