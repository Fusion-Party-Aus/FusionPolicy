# Expose Hidden Professional Campaign Support

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-13  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY

Political disclosure rules would expressly capture material donated or seconded professional campaign labour and organisational resources not already adequately reported, with consistent valuation and linked public records; ordinary unpaid personal volunteering would remain outside compulsory time reporting.

### The Problem

Professional labour can carry financial value even when it is supplied instead of cash. Queensland reform should identify genuine reporting gaps, use consistent valuation and protect ordinary unpaid participation from unnecessary political timesheets.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Cost disclosure-system changes, guidance, audit and enforcement. Assess campaign compliance burden. No revenue or spending reduction is assumed.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Electoral administration and compliance allocations. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Review existing coverage** — Review existing treatment of paid, donated and seconded staff, agency work and organisational campaign resources.
- **Define material support** — Define any additional reportable support by material value and professional or organisational supply, following legal review.
- **Disclose providers and value** — Publish provider identity, recipient, broad service category and valuation method where lawful and required.
- **Link reporting records** — Align reporting with existing disclosure systems and prevent duplicate reporting through linked records.
- **Protect ordinary volunteers** — Exempt ordinary unpaid personal volunteering from compulsory hour-by-hour public reporting and protect individual privacy.

### Research behind this policy

ECQ already requires real-time disclosure of qualifying party gifts and loans.

**Design Rationale**

Cash and professionally supplied work can serve the same campaign function. Queensland already has gift and loan disclosure duties, so a labour-specific rule must address a demonstrated gap rather than assume an exemption. The affected support is material professional or organisational provision, not all personal participation.

**System Interaction**

Paid, seconded and donated professional work need a consistent relationship to existing gift definitions. Provider identity, recipient and broad service category can identify the support without publishing personal volunteer histories. Linked records avoid counting the same contribution twice. Borderline mixed professional and personal activity can remain difficult to value even under a coherent rule; guidance and review must distinguish genuine uncertainty from deliberate misclassification.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Disclosure has compliance costs for campaigns and administrative costs for the regulator. Materiality rules determine whether reporting captures professional support without making ordinary volunteering impractical.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Apply equivalent treatment across business, union and other organisational providers. Keep an accessible correction and review process. Do not publish volunteer identities without a lawful basis.

**Evidence & Precedent**

ECQ already requires real-time disclosure of qualifying party gifts and loans. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Obtain current definitions, exemptions, valuation rules and audit evidence; settle any new threshold only after review.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: seek ECQ and constitutional advice; map actual reporting gaps. Months 6-18: consult parties and civil society on valuation and workable thresholds. Years 2-4: implement justified amendments, guidance and proportionate enforcement. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
