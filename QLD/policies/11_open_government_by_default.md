# Publish Routine Government Information

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-11  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY

Routine government disclosure obligations would set common publication requirements for decisions, spending, contracts, relevant meetings and service results, with accessible reusable formats, defined release timeframes and reviewable lawful exceptions.

### The Problem

Transparency should be part of normal administration. A useful system publishes decision reasons and spending in accessible formats, keeps records current and protects information only where a lawful and demonstrable reason applies.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Cost platform maintenance, records cleanup, staffing, privacy review and accessibility. Treat possible reductions in access-request workload as an evaluated benefit, not guaranteed savings.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Agency records-management and digital-service budgets. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Set publication standards** — Set a publication standard for contracts, project milestones, meeting records, decision reasons and aggregate service performance.
- **Disclose official meetings** — Publish ministerial and senior decision-maker meeting information under defined categories and proportionate timeframes.
- **Make information usable** — Provide searchable machine-readable data alongside plain-language summaries and accessible documents.
- **Explain exceptions** — Maintain an exceptions register explaining withheld categories, responsible decision-makers and review dates.
- **Fund records capacity** — Resource records management, publication officers and independent monitoring rather than shifting unfunded work to agencies.

### Research behind this policy

Queensland already has publication schemes and disclosure logs under its information-access framework. The reform is better routine release and usability, not creating proactive disclosure from nothing.

**Design Rationale**

Queensland already uses publication schemes and disclosure logs. The design question is how routine information reaches the public without separate requests, while preserving lawful confidentiality. Publication without usable records or current metadata does not provide equivalent access.

**System Interaction**

Privacy, security and investigation constraints determine exceptions; a general preference for confidentiality does not establish one. Review dates distinguish continuing restrictions from information that can later be released. Accessible summaries and reusable records serve different audiences within the same obligation. A privacy-versus-detail trade-off remains in some releases even where exceptions are correctly applied; records management must preserve a lawful review trail.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Routine publication requires records and privacy capacity. Reduced access-request work is a possible benefit to evaluate, rather than an assumed source of funding.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Protect personal information, victims, security and legitimate investigation needs. Apply lawful exemptions and review pathways; transparency does not authorise indiscriminate publication.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Queensland already has publication schemes and disclosure logs under its information-access framework. The reform is better routine release and usability, not creating proactive disclosure from nothing. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Inventory existing publication duties, identify system costs and determine which changes require legislation rather than administrative direction.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: audit publication practice and consult privacy and information-access bodies. Months 6-18: introduce a common standard and pilot high-value datasets. Years 2-4: expand coverage and publish independent compliance findings. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
