# Keep Strategic Assets in Public Hands

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-09  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC ASSETS

Major decisions about strategic public assets would require a published public-interest appraisal before disposal, concession extension or acquisition, with retained public ownership as the default and any departure supported by a whole-life assessment.

### The Problem

Ownership decisions should be judged by service quality, resilience, affordability and long-term returns. Public ownership is a policy preference for strategic assets, but acquisition, contract change or operation must be assessed honestly rather than presented as a guaranteed saving.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Include valuation, compensation, transaction costs, workforce transition, capital renewal and operating liabilities. A proposal to retain income also has financing and opportunity costs.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Relevant asset-owner and public-sector governance budgets; retention or acquisition requires an approved financing plan. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Register strategic assets** — Create a public register of major strategic assets, ownership, concession expiry dates and material state commitments.
- **Appraise major decisions** — Require public-interest appraisals before major disposals, long concessions or contract extensions.
- **Compare operating models** — Compare continued ownership, public operation, regulated contracting and other feasible models using consistent whole-life assumptions.
- **Publish public-interest findings** — Publish reasons and non-sensitive appraisal findings, including compensation, transition risk and foregone income.
- **Fund operational capability** — Require a funded capability and maintenance plan whenever public operation or acquisition is proposed.

### Research behind this policy

Queensland publishes a government procurement framework. Each asset also has its own governance and contractual setting, which must be examined individually.

**Design Rationale**

An upfront receipt is not equivalent to the long-term value of an asset. Ownership, service delivery and commercial risk can move separately under a concession. Comparison requires consistent treatment of retained income, capital renewal, financing and contingent liabilities.

**System Interaction**

Retained ownership is the default, not a claim that every acquisition is economical. Existing contractual rights remain relevant to compensation and timing. A public-interest appraisal tests feasible alternatives rather than comparing a fully costed private option against an uncosted public model. Valuation uncertainty and future demand remain live even with sound appraisal. This item governs ownership and concessions; transport operating-contract selection is distinct.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Retaining an income stream has financing and opportunity costs. A public ownership decision must compare long-run returns, liabilities and service obligations with the alternatives.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Respect lawful contracts and workers. Protect operationally sensitive information while explaining redactions. Do not assume every acquisition or change of operator improves outcomes.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Queensland publishes a government procurement framework. Each asset also has its own governance and contractual setting, which must be examined individually. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Obtain asset valuations, concession terms, revenue flows, capital backlogs and public operating capability.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: Treasury-led inventory and appraisal standards. Months 6-18: apply standards to approaching decisions and publish assessments. Years 2-4: implement justified changes with independent transition assurance. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
