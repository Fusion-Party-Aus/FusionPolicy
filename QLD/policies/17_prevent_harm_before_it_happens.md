# Fund Evaluated Crime Prevention

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-17  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC SERVICES

Community-safety funding would support coordinated local prevention programs with defined harm objectives, available victim support and independent outcome evaluation, with continuation tied to evidence rather than participation counts alone.

### The Problem

Enforcement is necessary, but lasting safety also depends on reducing repeat harm and addressing preventable risks. Queensland should coordinate local prevention with effective victim support and test outcomes rather than reward activity counts alone.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Cost coordination, frontline services and evaluation. Identify existing programs being integrated and any additional funding. Do not book avoided-crime savings before results are demonstrated.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Community-safety and prevention program allocations, with existing and additional funding distinguished. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Coordinate prevention plans** — Create local harm-reduction plans connecting policing, housing, youth support, health, schools and council services.
- **Prioritise victims and risks** — Prioritise repeat victims and demonstrated local risks, using de-identified data and safeguards.
- **Trial evaluated programs** — Fund time-limited prevention trials with a theory of change, baseline and independent evaluation.
- **Follow through on support** — Provide practical victim support and referral follow-through as part of each plan.
- **Publish and act on results** — Publish results, including ineffective programs, and redirect resources when improvement is not demonstrated.

**Design Rationale**

Program attendance does not establish a change in victimisation. Crime reporting, service access and harm can move differently. Evaluation needs a plausible comparison and a clear account of who the program reaches, rather than inferring success from activity alone.

**System Interaction**

QPS crime resources provide one part of a local baseline, not a complete measure of victim experience. The program objective needs a defined harm and a population appropriate to the intervention. Improved reporting can raise recorded incidents while underlying harm falls; this remains an attribution issue even where services operate correctly. This funding reform is distinct from the police operational model and the youth-specific referral pathway.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Prevention benefits may emerge over time and cannot be inferred from participation. Existing program spending and new service capacity must be distinguished before claiming avoided future costs.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Avoid demographic profiling or equating disadvantage with criminality. Protect victim information. Improved reporting may raise recorded incidents while safety improves.

**Evidence & Precedent**

QPS identifies public crime-data resources. Reported offences provide part of the baseline, but must be interpreted alongside reporting behaviour and other measures of victimisation. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Obtain program coverage, comparable baselines, service waitlists and victim-experience measures; establish evaluation feasibility.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: inventory existing programs and select contrasting pilot communities. Months 6-18: deliver coordinated pilots with independent evaluators. Years 2-4: assess outcomes against comparison areas and expand only justified approaches. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
