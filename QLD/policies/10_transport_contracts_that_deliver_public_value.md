# Test Transport Contracts Before Renewal

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-10  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC CONTRACTS

Significant transport operating-contract renewals would require published comparisons of feasible public and contracted provision, using consistent lifecycle costs and passenger-service requirements, with auditable performance obligations retained in the selected model.

### The Problem

The right operating model cannot be settled by a slogan. Queensland should compare feasible public and contracted options before renewal, align incentives with passenger outcomes and maintain an integrated network regardless of operator.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Include procurement, legal review, transition, depots, fleet finance and retained state risk. No billion-dollar saving or automatic franchise-profit recovery is assumed.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Transport procurement and operating budgets; any transition funding requires separate approval. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Publish contract decisions** — Publish a forward register of significant transport contract decisions and non-sensitive payment and performance information.
- **Compare operating models** — Before renewal, compare public operation and competitive regulated provision on consistent lifecycle assumptions.
- **Reward service delivery** — Use contract incentives for delivered services, accessibility, reliability and coordinated passenger information.
- **Audit performance** — Require auditable reporting of cancellations, payments, penalties and material service changes.
- **Protect network integration** — Preserve fare and timetable integration; plan workforce and passenger transitions before any operator change.

### Research behind this policy

Queensland has a procurement framework and Translink timetable data. Contract-level payments, risks and performance need additional disclosure and analysis.

**Design Rationale**

Contract payments cannot be compared sensibly without matching fleet, depot, maintenance and risk responsibilities. A lower quoted operating fee may leave costs with the state. The comparison must concern the same passenger service and a feasible operating capability.

**System Interaction**

Fare, timetable and information integration remain conditions of the selected model. Performance obligations distinguish scheduled services from delivered services and separate operational penalties from changes to the agreed network. Workers and passengers need transition arrangements within the contract decision. Forecast costs retain uncertainty even after like-for-like appraisal; published sensitivity analysis can expose that uncertainty without claiming a guaranteed saving.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Contract prices do not capture every cost borne by the state. Fleet finance, depots, transition liabilities and retained risks must be included when comparing operating models.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Do not cherry-pick high-demand routes or weaken employment and safety standards. Protect lawful confidentiality narrowly and explain what remains withheld.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Queensland has a procurement framework and Translink timetable data. Contract-level payments, risks and performance need additional disclosure and analysis. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Obtain contracts, payments, risk allocation, fleet ownership and consistent operator-level performance data.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: identify renewal dates and data gaps; agree assessment standards. Months 6-18: independently appraise priority contracts and consult users and workers. Years 2-4: introduce justified contract changes and publish passenger outcomes. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
