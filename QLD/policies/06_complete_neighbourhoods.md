# Bring Services into New Neighbourhoods

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-06  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PLANNING RULES

Major growth-area planning would require costed service-sequencing agreements that connect housing occupation with transport, utility and essential local-service delivery, naming responsible authorities and lawful funding obligations.

### The Problem

Housing delivery and service delivery should be planned together. Sequencing transport, utilities, safe paths and community facilities can reduce dependence on long car trips, while flexible local land uses allow shops and services to emerge as communities grow.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Cost early services, coordination and infrastructure delivery. Allocate state, council and proponent responsibilities transparently, testing impacts on housing prices and project viability.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — State infrastructure and service budgets, council programs and lawful proponent contributions. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Sequence services** — Require a costed service-sequencing plan for major growth areas, naming responsible agencies, funding and staged delivery triggers.
- **Allow local uses** — Permit appropriate small shops, community uses and diverse housing through clear local standards and infrastructure capacity tests.
- **Connect new residents** — Coordinate initial public transport and safe walking links with occupation of major residential stages.
- **Publish capacity assumptions** — Publish school, health, water, sewer and community-facility capacity assumptions, with updates when development timing changes.
- **Fund early services** — Use early service funding and staged infrastructure agreements to close gaps without imposing conditions unrelated to a development.

**Design Rationale**

Residential development and public services have different funding and construction cycles. An approval may assume capacity or future services that are not yet funded. Sequencing agreements address the relationship between occupation and service readiness rather than treating every facility as a developer obligation.

**System Interaction**

State agencies, councils, utilities and proponents retain their lawful responsibilities. Agreements must identify both the planned service and the party responsible for funding it. Conditions cannot substitute for powers an authority does not hold. Development timing can change even where commitments are fulfilled, leaving a trade-off between early service provision and temporary underuse. This item changes sequencing; housing-permission rules remain a separate reform surface.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Service sequencing distributes costs between infrastructure owners and service providers. Earlier provision may require public spending before a growing neighbourhood produces its full revenue base.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Maintain flood, biodiversity and accessibility safeguards. Avoid blanket moratoria or retrospective conditions that delay viable homes. Consult Traditional Owners and local residents early.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Queensland planning operates under its own state and local planning framework. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Obtain growth staging, utility capacity, service lead times, lawful contribution mechanisms and agency delivery commitments.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: review legal powers, existing infrastructure plans and priority growth-area gaps. Months 6-18: pilot coordinated service agreements with councils and state agencies. Years 2-4: incorporate evaluated requirements into relevant planning instruments and funding programs. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
