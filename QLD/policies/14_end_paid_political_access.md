# End Paid Political Access

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-14  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY

Political fundraising rules would prohibit selling privileged access to ministers, MPs or candidates and require disclosure of relevant access arrangements, while preserving ordinary fundraising and open unpaid stakeholder engagement.

### The Problem

Political fundraising and public engagement should be separable. Queensland can develop clear rules against selling privileged access while maintaining legitimate small-donor fundraising and open discussion with citizens, businesses, unions and community groups.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Cost regulator staffing, guidance, reporting systems and enforcement. Publish the fiscal effect separately from the political benefit; no direct budget savings are assumed.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Electoral and integrity administration allocations. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Prohibit purchased access** — Define and prohibit fundraising arrangements that sell privileged access to ministers, MPs or candidates, subject to constitutional advice.
- **Disclose access events** — Publish relevant paid-access event details and material policy representations under a consistent transparency standard.
- **Separate decisions and fundraising** — Separate official meetings and government decisions from fundraising operations and document conflicts of interest.
- **Open stakeholder channels** — Maintain open, non-paid channels for stakeholder representations and ordinary community engagement.
- **Independent enforcement** — Give the regulator clear guidance, proportionate enforcement powers and transparent review procedures for any new duties.

### Research behind this policy

Queensland already regulates reportable political gifts and loans. Whether specific event practices require additional restrictions needs legal and evidence review.

**Design Rationale**

A donation, attendance at an ordinary event and purchased privileged access are not interchangeable. A restriction needs a defined connection between payment and access, rather than an assumption that every fundraising interaction changes an official decision.

**System Interaction**

The prohibited transaction must be defined consistently across corporate, union, community and individual participants. Constitutional advice is necessary on political expression and association. Disclosure supports the access prohibition but does not itself establish misconduct. Informal social relationships remain outside a purchased-access test unless linked to consideration; that boundary remains even when the prohibition is effectively administered.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Restrictions on purchased access are an integrity measure rather than a budget-saving measure. Administration and enforcement need explicit funding.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Do not treat attendance at every fundraiser as corruption. Protect lawful political expression and ordinary fundraising. Findings require evidence and procedural fairness.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Queensland already regulates reportable political gifts and loans. Whether specific event practices require additional restrictions needs legal and evidence review. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Determine constitutional limits, existing event disclosure, regulatory powers and clear distinctions between ordinary fundraising and purchased privilege.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: review current rules and identify evidenced access-selling practices. Months 6-18: consult on workable definitions, rights and enforcement. Years 2-4: implement legally supported reforms and audit compliance. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
