# Trial Neighbourhood Police Posts

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-21  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC SERVICES

Local police access would be trialled through staffed neighbourhood posts or scheduled outreach at selected activity centres, with published availability, protected emergency-response capacity and independent comparison against other local service models.

### The Problem

Small police posts and foot or bicycle patrols may improve access and local relationships. They should be tested against other uses of scarce officers, rather than assumed to deliver safety simply because a new shopfront opens.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Cost leases, fit-out, accessibility, officers, security and ongoing operation. Compare with mobile services and existing capacity. New premises do not create additional officers.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Police workforce and facilities trial allocations. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Trial local access** — Trial staffed local posts or scheduled outreach at suitable transport hubs and activity centres.
- **Choose suitable sites** — Choose sites using community access, harm patterns, officer availability and comparative service options.
- **Publish availability** — Publish opening hours and purpose so residents understand when assistance is available and how to contact emergency services.
- **Connect local responses** — Pair local presence with lawful patrols, community problem-solving and reliable referral arrangements.
- **Compare service models** — Evaluate posts against mobile outreach and existing stations before permanent expansion.

**Design Rationale**

A shopfront changes the place police can be contacted, but does not create additional officers. Local access must be assessed against mobile outreach and existing stations. Crime and confidence are separate outcomes and neither should be inferred from the opening of premises alone.

**System Interaction**

Published availability defines the service residents can expect and distinguishes it from an emergency response facility. Confidential reporting needs privacy within the local premises. Emergency capacity constrains the trial design, but a remaining opportunity cost exists whenever officers are assigned to one form of presence rather than another. A site may improve access without demonstrating a measurable reduction in local crime.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

A local post changes how an existing workforce is deployed. Premises costs and officer time must be compared with mobile outreach and existing stations.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

No enforcement quotas or surveillance expansion by default. Provide private spaces for sensitive reports and maintain independent complaint access.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Queensland crime-data resources can support pilot selection, while the CCC provides existing police integrity oversight. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Obtain workforce availability, local demand, response-time baselines, site safety and comparative operating costs.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: select trial sites and assess staffing and emergency-response impacts. Months 6-18: operate pilots with clear hours, safeguards and evaluation. Years 2-4: retain effective posts, relocate or close weak trials and publish the reasons. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
