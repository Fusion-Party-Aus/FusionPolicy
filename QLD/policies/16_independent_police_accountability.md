# Require Independent Police Accountability

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-16  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY

Police complaint and serious-incident oversight would be subject to explicit independence, case-update and review requirements, with funded oversight capacity and de-identified reporting within Queensland's existing integrity framework.

### The Problem

Police exercise significant powers. Accountability protects the community, honest officers and public confidence. Queensland reform should examine investigation independence and capacity, rather than assume that creating another organisation will automatically improve oversight.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Cost investigators, legal advice, data systems and complainant support. Establish independent capacity before transferring functions; avoid uncosted duplication.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Independent integrity oversight allocations, with police and investigation responsibilities explicitly funded. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Review independence** — Independently review complaint triage, investigative independence, referrals, resourcing and access for vulnerable complainants.
- **Set handling standards** — Set public standards for case updates and timely handling, with escalation for serious or conflicted matters.
- **Publish oversight data** — Publish de-identified data on use of powers, complaints, findings and remedies with clear definitions.
- **Protect evidence and privacy** — Review body-worn camera governance, evidence retention and access arrangements against privacy and fair-process requirements.
- **Fund justified improvements** — Resource justified oversight improvements and evaluate their effect on confidence and substantiated misconduct.

**Design Rationale**

Oversight credibility depends on jurisdiction, investigative independence and the capacity to act. Queensland already has a police integrity oversight body. The institutional question is whether existing arrangements can meet defined independence requirements, not whether another organisation is inherently preferable.

**System Interaction**

Serious and conflicted matters need an independence test that distinguishes oversight from an investigation controlled by the subject agency. Fair process remains necessary for officers and complainants. Reporting must separate allegations, findings and remedies. Confidential evidence can limit what is explained publicly even under independent oversight; that restriction should not be mistaken for a substantiated finding or absence of review.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Independent oversight requires investigative, legal and reporting capacity. Reallocating functions between bodies can change costs without creating additional capacity.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Protect victims, complainants and officers from prejudgment. Maintain due process and lawful investigation confidentiality. Rising complaints alone do not prove rising misconduct.

**Evidence & Precedent**

The CCC is Queensland's police integrity oversight body and has a role in complaints and serious police-related incidents. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Review current powers, case flow, investigative referrals, delays and resourcing before choosing an institutional model.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: commission an independent review with community and officer participation. Months 6-18: implement administrative improvements and cost legislative options. Years 2-4: introduce justified changes and publish independent evaluation. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
