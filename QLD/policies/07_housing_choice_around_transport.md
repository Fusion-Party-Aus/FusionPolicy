# Allow More Homes Near Public Transport

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-07  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PLANNING RULES

Planning schemes would incorporate a Queensland housing code permitting diverse housing and compatible local uses in defined serviced transport precincts, with objective design, infrastructure and hazard requirements and explicit terms for any affordability-linked incentives.

### The Problem

Predictable planning can support duplexes, terraces, townhouses, small apartments and compatible local uses. The appropriate scale depends on infrastructure and actual access. More supply must be accompanied by a separate enforceable affordability mechanism where public benefits are offered.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Cost precinct utilities, public realm, staffing and transport services. Model incentive value and developer viability. Publish any public subsidy and foregone revenue separately.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Planning, transport and infrastructure budgets, with lawful contributions assessed precinct by precinct. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Set housing standards** — Develop a Queensland model code for diverse housing in suitable serviced areas, with clear design and infrastructure tests.
- **Map useful catchments** — Map precincts using actual walking connections and service quality, rather than one universal station radius.
- **Plan mixed precincts** — Coordinate homes, shops, employment, public space and accessible paths around major transport hubs.
- **Test affordability measures** — Test enforceable affordable-housing obligations or incentives, specifying eligibility, rent limits, duration and monitoring.
- **Publish infrastructure plans** — Publish precinct infrastructure capacity and delivery plans before granting additional development capacity.

### Research behind this policy

Queensland has its own planning framework. Station-area permissions and infrastructure responsibilities require review of state instruments and individual council schemes.

**Design Rationale**

Permitted housing forms and feasible housing projects are not the same. Clear permissions can remove a legal barrier, but utilities, land assembly, construction and finance still determine delivery. Precinct boundaries need to reflect usable transport access and capacity rather than treating proximity alone as sufficient.

**System Interaction**

Objective requirements preserve mapped hazard and environmental constraints without allowing unrelated discretionary barriers to recreate the original restriction. Affordability-linked incentives need defined beneficiaries, rent or price treatment, duration and enforceability; extra density alone is not an affordability guarantee. This code changes permissions, not the separate service-sequencing agreements or public ownership of homes. Market financing constraints remain even where the code operates correctly.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Permission for more homes and delivery of affordable homes are different institutional results. Utility capacity, development viability and public subsidy need separate treatment.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Keep flood, heat, accessibility and ecological safeguards. Assess displacement and construction impacts. Additional density is not assumed to deliver affordability automatically.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Queensland has its own planning framework. Station-area permissions and infrastructure responsibilities require review of state instruments and individual council schemes. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Obtain site capacity, infrastructure costs, lawful planning mechanisms and feasibility evidence before setting numeric obligations.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: undertake statutory review and identify candidate precincts with councils. Months 6-18: consult on model standards, appraise infrastructure and test affordability mechanisms. Years 2-4: adopt suitable instruments, monitor completions and revise standards where outcomes disappoint. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
