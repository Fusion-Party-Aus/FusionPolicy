# Let Communities Decide Local Spending

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-22  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR COMMUNITY VOICE

Defined discretionary local investment budgets would be allocated through voluntary state-council participatory budgeting pilots, with published project rules, supported resident participation, conflict disclosure and decisions confined to lawful authority.

### The Problem

Participatory budgeting can make priorities visible and improve local ownership. Queensland should start with clearly funded pilots and defined decision boundaries, ensuring that organised groups do not crowd out residents who have less time or confidence to participate.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Publish the allocated project budget plus facilitation, outreach, feasibility and maintenance costs. Explain which discretionary spending is redirected and protect essential statutory services.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — A defined existing discretionary project budget, plus separately funded participation and administration. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Define the budget** — Create voluntary state-council pilots for a defined, published discretionary investment envelope.
- **Invite feasible projects** — Invite residents to propose projects and publish eligibility, costs, maintenance responsibilities and delivery constraints.
- **Support participation** — Provide supported participation through meetings, accessible digital options and outreach to underrepresented groups.
- **Transparent selection** — Use transparent selection rules, conflict declarations and independent facilitation; explain how decisions affect the budget.
- **Publish delivery and review** — Publish selected projects, rejected proposals with reasons, delivery progress and independent evaluation.

### Research behind this policy

Queensland's planning framework allocates formal responsibilities to public authorities. Community participation cannot silently replace statutory decision-making or elected accountability.

**Design Rationale**

Consultation can invite preferences without identifying what residents are authorised to decide. A defined budget and project boundary make that authority intelligible. Participation must be assessed by who is reached, not only by the number of responses received.

**System Interaction**

The pilot envelope excludes decisions outside lawful delegation and does not transfer statutory planning approval powers. Project eligibility needs lifecycle maintenance responsibility, not only construction cost. Supported participation and conflicts management address access and capture within the same allocation process. Majority preference can still differ from minority needs even when participation is representative; statutory rights remain outside the spending vote.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Participatory budgeting changes who selects eligible projects within a defined envelope. Facilitation and maintenance costs are additional to the visible construction or purchase price.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Protect minority rights and avoid political capture. Do not delegate approvals beyond lawful powers. Account for people without digital access and those unable to attend meetings.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Queensland's planning framework allocates formal responsibilities to public authorities. Community participation cannot silently replace statutory decision-making or elected accountability. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Determine lawful delegation, council agreement, eligible expenditure, maintenance liability and a feasible inclusive selection process.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: agree pilot partners, legal boundaries and a funded envelope. Months 6-18: run proposal development and selection with community support. Years 2-4: deliver selected projects, evaluate inclusion and consider expansion. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
