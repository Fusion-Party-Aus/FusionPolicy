# Solve Recurring Local Safety Problems

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-18  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC SERVICES

Police responses to recurring local harm would use a documented problem-analysis model that links proportionate tactics and relevant partner actions to assessed causes, with evaluation of harm, displacement and use-of-power effects.

### The Problem

A recurring safety problem may involve poor lighting, a dangerous crossing, repeat victimisation or a particular location. Queensland should analyse the problem, choose proportionate responses and check whether harm falls rather than rely on indiscriminate enforcement.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Cost officer training, analysis, partner works and independent evaluation. Check whether local initiatives divert capacity from emergency response.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Police operations and analysis allocations, plus separately approved partner works. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Diagnose recurring problems** — Use a documented process to identify recurring problems, analyse causes, respond and assess results.
- **Coordinate proportionate responses** — Combine lawful targeted policing with council, venue, transport and service changes where the evidence supports them.
- **Involve affected people** — Engage affected residents, victims, businesses and relevant community organisations in defining the problem.
- **Check displacement** — Track harm in nearby areas to identify displacement rather than declaring success from one location alone.
- **Publish evaluation** — Publish de-identified problem plans, evaluation findings and reasons for changing approach.

**Design Rationale**

The location of reported incidents does not itself identify their cause. A response may shift activity nearby rather than reduce harm. A specific-problem model therefore requires attention to context and neighbouring areas, not just totals at the intervention site.

**System Interaction**

Lawful powers constrain tactics; a problem plan does not create an additional power to search, stop or surveil. Partner works need to address the same diagnosed harm rather than create independent program commitments. Displacement can remain even where a targeted response works as designed because people and activities can move. Assessment must consider nearby impacts alongside the chosen site.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Analysis and targeted partner works use resources that could otherwise serve other police and community functions. Evaluation needs to consider this opportunity cost alongside changes in local harm.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

No quotas for stops or searches. Use lawful powers proportionately and assess discriminatory effects. Do not publish information identifying victims or sensitive locations.

**Evidence & Precedent**

QPS makes aggregated crime information available through its information resources. Local diagnosis also needs incident context, community input and safeguards against misleading small numbers. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Obtain incident-level quality checks, lawful data-sharing arrangements and feasible comparison areas before selecting tactics.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: agree selection and civil-liberties criteria; choose pilot locations. Months 6-18: train teams, deliver responses and monitor comparison areas. Years 2-4: retain effective methods and discontinue or redesign harmful or ineffective tactics. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
