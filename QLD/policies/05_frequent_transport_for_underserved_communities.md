# Expand Frequent Transport in Underserved Areas

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-05  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC SERVICES

Public transport service allocation would use published access assessments to direct staged operating investment to underserved corridors, combining frequent fixed routes with connecting services and locally evaluated alternatives.

### The Problem

A low fare has limited value without a service that runs when people need it. Queensland should rank underserved communities by population, essential destinations and disadvantage, then fund practical connections instead of assuming every place needs the same mode.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Publish recurring driver, fleet, fuel or energy and maintenance costs as well as stop and priority works. Secure operating funding before promising a network. No route or farebox revenue assumption substitutes for a business case.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Additional public transport operating allocations and supporting capital programs. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Map service gaps** — Publish maps of service frequency, operating hours and realistic walking access, with missing-data areas clearly identified.
- **Rank improvements** — Rank improvements using resident need, disadvantage, growth, essential destinations and achievable network benefits.
- **Connect trunk routes and feeders** — Develop frequent trunk buses with dependable feeders and safe interchange access; appraise bus priority where delays undermine the service.
- **Use suitable regional models** — Use regional service designs suited to local demand, including evaluated flexible options where frequent fixed routes are unsuitable.
- **Publish funded service plans** — Publish corridor service plans, recurrent funding and outcomes, separating current commitments from proposed expansions.

**Design Rationale**

An affordable fare does not resolve a missing service or a service operating outside useful hours. Access assessment needs the location of residents, essential destinations and actual walking connections. A single urban frequency threshold is not an adequate description of need across all regional settings.

**System Interaction**

Timetable data measure promised service, while observed reliability requires separate evidence. Areas lacking usable data must be distinguished from areas verified to lack service. Patronage is relevant but cannot alone capture essential access for small or disadvantaged populations. Demand responses and development timing remain uncertain even with a functioning allocation process, so access outcomes and operating costs require joint evaluation.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Frequent service requires recurrent operating funding, not only new stops or vehicles. Low patronage can reflect poor access as well as low demand, so allocation needs both access and cost evidence.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Include shift workers, carers, young people and disabled passengers. Do not equate unavailable data with zero service or rank sparsely populated communities only by patronage.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Queensland timetable data supports assessment of scheduled services. A timetable score does not measure actual punctuality; Queensland baselines must be built and labelled accordingly. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Confirm current timetables, population distribution, walkable paths, fleet availability and route-level operating costs.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: establish the Queensland access baseline and identify candidate corridors. Months 6-18: consult communities and fund staged service increases with operational capacity secured. Years 2-4: expand successful corridors, redesign weak services and refresh access maps. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
