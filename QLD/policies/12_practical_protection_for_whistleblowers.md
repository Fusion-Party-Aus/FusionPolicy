# Protect People Who Report Wrongdoing

Working draft v0.3 - 7 October 2026. Queensland adaptations in the Victorian policy-page layout; not endorsed Fusion policy. Costs and funding routes are provisional and require Queensland appraisal.

**Policy ID:** QLD-12  
**Jurisdiction:** Queensland, Australia  
**Status:** Proposed policy draft  
**Policy surface:** OUR PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY

Public-interest disclosure protections would include confidential independent advice, safe referral between responsible bodies, assessed reprisal protection and reviewable responses to retaliation, while preserving fair investigation of allegations.

### The Problem

Formal protection is only useful if a person can understand how to report and obtain help when reprisals begin. Queensland should test protection in practice, while preserving fair investigation and the rights of people who are accused.

### What it costs

**Queensland estimate: not yet costed.** Cost specialist advice, case management, investigations and any remedy scheme. Fund independent capacity explicitly; potential recovered losses cannot be assumed to finance the service.

**Paid for by:** Proposed route — Public-sector integrity and whistleblower-protection allocations. Funding is subject to appraisal and budget approval.

### What it does

- **Independent advice** — Provide confidential independent advice on disclosure pathways, eligibility and available protection.
- **Safe referrals** — Create clear referral arrangements so a disclosure sent to the wrong body is handled safely and promptly.
- **Assess reprisal risk** — Require documented reprisal-risk assessment and a support plan when a qualifying disclosure is received.
- **Effective protection and remedies** — Review urgent protective measures and remedies for substantiated retaliation, supported by legal and employment advice.
- **Evaluate outcomes** — Publish de-identified performance measures and independent evaluation of protection, referrals and outcomes.

**Design Rationale**

A statutory protection and an accessible remedy are different parts of reporting safety. A person may not know which body has jurisdiction or whether their disclosure qualifies. Protection must therefore be assessed against access to advice, safe referral and a usable response when reprisal is alleged.

**System Interaction**

Queensland has an existing public-interest disclosure system. Eligibility, review powers and retaliation remedies need current statutory assessment. Confidential reporting is not a general authorisation for unrestricted publication. Fair investigation protects both reporters and people accused of wrongdoing. An independent protection process can address reprisals but cannot guarantee that every allegation is substantiated or restore every consequence of workplace conflict.

**Economic & Institutional Logic**

Independent advice and reprisal protection require funded capacity regardless of whether a disclosure later recovers public money. Remedies and investigation costs belong in the institutional design.

**Risk & Failure Modes**

Preserve confidentiality and procedural fairness. Distinguish protected disclosure from unrestricted public release. Do not publish allegations as findings or promise outcomes outside legal powers.

**Evidence & Precedent**

Queensland has a public-interest disclosure system administered through existing institutions. Queensland-specific evidence still required: Check current statutory coverage, review rights, remedy powers and evidence on reprisal and delays before final drafting.

**Implementation Outline**

A provisional sequence for appraisal is set out in the [Delivery Plan](../Fusion_Queensland_Delivery_Plan.md). First 6 months: review current legislation, agency practice and advice-service capacity. Months 6-18: fund practical support and trial prompt reprisal triage. Years 2-4: legislate justified gaps and evaluate protection outcomes. These stages are planning assumptions, not approved deadlines.
